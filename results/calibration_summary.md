# Calibration report — case 1

**What was measured.** For each of the 20 pairs in
`data/embedding_calibration_testcases.csv`, the cosine between the embedding of `text_a`
and of `text_b` on the same row, for every model and instruction mode. Generated by
`src/score_calibration.py`; full numbers in `calibration_report.xlsx` and
`calibration_report.csv`.

Both sides of a pair get identical treatment within a mode — bare in `no_instruct`,
prefixed in `instruct`. This is a symmetric similarity test, not retrieval. The T0
sanity pair (two identical strings) reads **1.000 in every column**, which is the
evidence that the pipeline and the vectors are sound.

## Results

Mean cosine per tier, ordered as the ladder is supposed to fall.

| model / mode | T0 identical | T1 paraphrase | T5 vocab gap | T2 **same doc, other attribute** | T3 same topic, other doc | T4 unrelated | **T1−T2** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3_0.6b / no_instruct | 1.000 | 0.855 | 0.643 | 0.706 | 0.657 | 0.159 | **0.149** |
| qwen3_0.6b / instruct | 1.000 | 0.924 | 0.725 | 0.824 | 0.691 | 0.406 | 0.100 |
| qwen3_vl_2b / no_instruct | 1.000 | 0.886 | 0.737 | 0.745 | 0.744 | 0.293 | 0.141 |
| qwen3_vl_2b / instruct | 1.000 | 0.919 | 0.672 | 0.787 | 0.719 | 0.401 | 0.131 |

| **text3large** | 1.000 | 0.857 | 0.629 | 0.674 | 0.744 | 0.304 | **0.182** |

## Findings

**1. Every model fails the vocabulary-gap rung.** T5 pairs say the same thing in
completely different words and should score near T1. Instead they land *below* T2 in all
six configurations, `text3large` included. In production this means a passage that
answers the user's question in different wording ranks below a passage from the right
document carrying the wrong attribute. That is precisely the case dense retrieval is
supposed to win over BM25, and it is the weakest rung for every model tested. It is also
the strongest argument here for keeping BM25 in a hybrid rather than going dense-only.

**2. `text3large` separates T1 from T2 best** — 0.182 raw, and 25.3% of its own cosine
range against roughly 16–17% for the others. Distinguishing "the answer, reworded" from
"same document, wrong attribute" is the property that decides whether a similarity
threshold can be trusted, and it leads by a clear margin.

**3. Two models cannot tell documents apart.** `vn_embedding` and `text3large` both fail
`T2 > T3`: a *different* decision (205/QĐ-TCT vs 198/QĐ-TCT) scores higher than the same
decision discussing another attribute. Both Qwen models pass this rung in both modes.
So the two leaders lead on different things — `text3large` on attribute separation,
Qwen on document identity.

**4. The instruction prefix compresses the scale without helping.** For both Qwen models
`instruct` lifts every tier, but lifts the low tiers most: T4 (unrelated) goes 0.159 →
0.406 for `qwen3_0.6b`, and the usable cosine range shrinks from 0.895 to 0.615. The
T1−T2 gap gets *worse* for `qwen3_0.6b` (0.149 → 0.100) and slightly worse for
`qwen3_vl_2b` (0.141 → 0.131). No rung is repaired in either case. On this evidence the
prefix is not worth using for a symmetric similarity task — though this says nothing
about retrieval, where the prefix is applied to the query alone and is the documented
usage.

**5. `qwen3_vl_2b` does not beat `qwen3_0.6b` here** despite being three times the size
and 2048 dims against 1024. Its `no_instruct` T1−T2 is 0.141 vs 0.149. Note its
`no_instruct` is not strictly comparable: this model wraps every input in a default
`Represent the user's input.` system prompt, so its "bare" mode still carries an
instruction.

## What this does not establish

20 pairs, and only 3 of them at T5, 5 at T1 and 5 at T2. Differences between models of
a few hundredths are inside the noise of a sample this small — the T1−T2 ordering of
`text3large` over the rest is the only gap wide enough to lean on, and even that would
want ~30 pairs per tier to be called firm. Finding 1 is stated with more confidence than
the others only because it is unanimous across six independent configurations.

This measures pair similarity, not retrieval. Nothing here reports how often a model
puts the right chunk at rank 1 against the 2,038-chunk corpus; that is the retrieval
benchmark, still waiting on a golden set.

`ada002` has no calibration vectors — those 40 short texts were never sent to the
OpenAI API. `text3large`'s column comes from `data/results_detail_v1_pairs.csv`, scored
elsewhere; its texts were verified to match this CSV pair for pair before merging.
